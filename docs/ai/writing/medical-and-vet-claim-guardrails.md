# Medical and Vet Claim Guardrails

Defines what chill-dogs.com can and cannot claim about veterinary authority or medical efficacy.

## Use this when

Writing any product description, article, marketing copy, newsletter, or Pinterest pin that touches on health, anxiety, safety, or veterinary topics.

---

## Chill-Dogs is not a veterinary authority

The site is written by a careful, practical dog owner who researches and curates products. It is not:

- A veterinary publication
- Medically reviewed by veterinarians
- Sourced from veterinary consultants

Do not imply otherwise in any copy.

---

## Do not imply vet consultation or medical endorsement

Prohibited framings:

- "Vet-recommended" (without documented sourcing)
- "Vet-approved" (without documented sourcing)
- "Clinically proven"
- "Medically endorsed"
- "Our vet recommends"
- Any implication that Chill-Dogs commissioned veterinary reviews, gathered vet endorsements, or had a vet evaluate the products

If a product genuinely has documented veterinary endorsement and you can cite the source, that claim is allowed — but only with the citation.

---

## Substitution table

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| vet-approved | researched / commonly used / popular among dog owners |
| vet-recommended | widely used / a popular choice / frequently mentioned in owner communities |
| clinically proven | designed to / intended to (unless citing a real study with a link) |
| proven to reduce anxiety | intended to help dogs feel calmer / many owners report improvement |
| safe for all dogs | safe for most dogs — check with your vet if your dog has health conditions |
| expert-endorsed | based on our research / curated from owner feedback and product specifications |
| medically reviewed | research-based / sourced from named authorities (AKC, AAHA, etc.) |

---

## Emergency escalation language

Articles may describe symptoms and general first-response guidance. They must not replace veterinary advice.

When covering health emergencies, include a clear escalation statement:

**Required for:** heatstroke, seizure, breathing distress, collapse, severe anxiety, poisoning, any life-threatening symptom.

**Template:**
> "If your dog shows signs of [heatstroke / a seizure / severe breathing distress], contact your veterinarian or an emergency animal hospital immediately."

Do not suggest dosages, medications, or specific treatments. Do not frame any information as medical advice.

---

## Emergency topics that always require escalation language

- Heatstroke / hyperthermia
- Seizure or collapse
- Breathing distress or respiratory issues
- Severe anxiety or panic episodes
- Toxic ingestion

---

## Sourcing standards

When citing health information:

- Name the source: "According to the AKC," "AAHA recommends," "per a 2023 study published in..."
- Do not cite Wikipedia as a primary source.
- Hedge appropriately when information is consensus-based: "Most vets recommend..." is acceptable when it reflects genuine consensus — not when fabricating an endorsement.
- Specific figures add credibility and reduce legal risk: "85 °F on asphalt can reach 135 °F" is better than "asphalt gets very hot."
