### Safety and field kit

- [ ] 2 adults are present; do not run separation scenarios solo.
- [ ] Rhys has a secure, correctly fitted harness.
- [ ] Long line is inspected and attached to the harness, not solely to the tracker collar.
- [ ] Identification tags and microchip details are current.
- [ ] Test route or controlled area is known and legal to use.
- [ ] Backup phone, offline map/navigation, and power bank are available.
- [ ] Water, bowl, waste bags, treats, and first-aid supplies are packed.
- [ ] Both devices are charged and paired.
- [ ] No unsafe traffic, wildlife, livestock, weather, fire, or trail condition is present.
- [ ] Both people agree on voice/hand signals and the stop command.

### Stop immediately if

- Rhys shows distress, overheating, pain, unusual fatigue, rubbing, or equipment avoidance.
- The harness, collar, attachment, or long line loosens, tangles, snags, or appears damaged.
- Traffic, wildlife, another dog, a person, weather, terrain, or visibility creates risk.
- Either person loses reliable communication or visual control of the safe setup.
- Testing would require intentionally releasing Rhys or exceeding the controlled area's limits.

## Roles

### Rhys handler

- Keeps Rhys secured and monitors comfort, behavior, equipment fit, and surroundings.
- Moves or holds Rhys only on the agreed signal.
- Calls the stop command at the first safety or welfare concern.
- Does not coach the receiver operator toward Rhys unless the scenario calls for it.

### Receiver operator / logger

- Carries the handheld as a first-time user would.
- Announces readings without receiving hidden position hints.
- Records timestamps, device readings, uncertainty, signal state, and recovery behavior.
- Captures display and terrain photos without delaying a safety response.
